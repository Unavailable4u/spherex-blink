    How this works

    Abstract. SPHEREx scans the whole sky in 102 infrared colours. Every field is seen on several dates, so a world that moves between visits is easy to catch: this is the blink technique that found Pluto in 1930.

    1. Pick a band and drag the time bar. Stars stay put; movers jump.
    2. Press Space to blink two frames, or O to overlay them in amber and cyan. The tool picks a sensible pair; change B with Set B.
    3. Click a source. It snaps to the centre and checks whether it stays put in the other frames. Then flag it.
    4. Open Context to see this field against optical, infrared and Hubble views, and on the whole sky.
    5. Open Planet X to see what a distant planet would do here, and to measure a mover's rate and distance.

    What this is, honestly

    • A working prototype for the Planet X and SPHEREx challenge, built on real QR2 spectral images.
    • It does not claim a discovery. A flag is a candidate for follow-up; the distance estimator says candidate, never found.
    • Tiles are 8-bit stretched pictures, so brightness readouts are approximate. Frames in one band sit at different wavelengths, so judge movers by position, not brightness.
    • Some frames show a broad diffuse band of glow. That is large-scale background in the detector images, not a point source.
    • Flags are shared only if the site is wired to a shared store; the panel says which mode is active.
